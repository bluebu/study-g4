  1. 1第 7 页

    My mother, Duchess, was a favorite of our master, and she always advised me to be gentle and kind, emphasizing the importance of not biting or kicking even during playtime. Her words touched me, and I made sure to always keep them in mind.

  2. 2第 7 页

    There was another event that I will always remember. One day, George Gordon, the son of Squire Gordon, the richest man in our area, fell off his horse and died while chasing a rabbit.

  3. 3第 7–8 页

    When my mother and I heard the news, we felt sad. We couldn't understand why humans hunted animals. But little did I know that soon I would leave my comfortable home and discover more about their strange ways.

  4. 4第 8 页

    When I was about four years old, Squire Gordon came to see me and showed his interest in me. He said I was the right horse for him and asked Master Farmer Grey to let him know when I was ready. The next day, my training started under Farmer Grey's guidance.

  5. 5第 8 页

    One morning, Squire Gordon bought me because he thought I was a beautiful horse. His head groom, James, led me to my new home, Birtwick Park. There, I discovered that Squire Gordon owned two other horses. One of them was Merrylegs, a friendly horse, while the other was Ginger, who was not as friendly. Ginger had a habit of biting and kicking people, which made the young ladies afraid to visit the stable. As a result, Ginger was moved to another stall. Merrylegs warned me about Ginger's behavior and hoped that I wouldn't bite.

  6. 6第 9 页

    Life at Birtwick Park was similar to my time at Farmer Grey's, but there were also noticeable differences. One major change was being paired with a new partner, Ginger, when pulling the carriage. I was worried about getting along with Ginger because Merrylegs had warned me about her bad temper. However, as we worked together several times, I discovered that she was an honest partner and we eventually became friends.

  7. 7第 9 页

    Another highlight was when all the horses gathered in the orchard. It was there that I discovered each horse had their own remarkable yet sorrowful stories to share, especially Ginger.

  8. 8第 10 页

    Her previous master was unkind. Some mischievous boys annoyed the horses by throwing things at them, but her master didn't care at all.

  9. 9第 10 页

    The trainers struck her without warning and never communicated their intentions before demanding something from her. The most painful memories for Ginger were the times she was sold. Each time, the dealers treated her even worse than before.

  10. 10第 10 页

    Merrylegs was a favorite among the children, especially the squire's daughters, Jessie and Flora. They loved riding him in the orchard. One day, a groom scolded Merrylegs for his behavior with the children. Feeling curious, I asked him what happened.

  11. 11第 11 页

    One of my favorite tasks at Birtwick Park was carrying the mistress when the whole family rode together. The young girls would take turns riding Merrylegs and Sir Oliver, while the squire would usually ride Ginger. I knew that the mistress liked riding me more than Ginger because I was easier to handle and more comfortable for riding. This made Ginger feel jealous of me.

  12. 12第 11 页

    "Don't be so hard on yourself," I comforted her. "You do a wonderful job for the squire. What we expected was to be treated kindly."

  13. 13第 12 页

    The squire always tried to stop cruelty toward animals. Once, we saw a man mistreating a pony. The squire stopped him, reminding him that the pony was a living being with feelings. The man stopped whipping the pony and left. I felt grateful to have such a kind master.

  14. 14第 12 页

    One autumn day, the squire and I went on a journey in a cart. The weather was cool, and the cart was light. As we traveled, we saw puddles and leaves blowing in the wind.

  15. 15第 12 页

    When we returned to the toll bridge, I hesitated. I did not move because the bridge felt strange and there must be something wrong. Then we were informed that the bridge was broken.

  16. 16第 13–14 页

    One day, the squire visited the stable while John was grooming me and James was getting oats. The squire looked serious, holding a letter in his hand. John quickly led me into the stall and approached the squire.

  17. 17第 14 页

    The squire explained that his brother-in-law needed a new groom, for his old coachman would be retired and he wanted a young man to take over. It was a good job with good pay and a nice home, and James wanted to take this chance. Everything was ready except the driving experience, so James had to practice driving the carriage as much as he could.

  18. 18第 14 页

    At sunset, we reached a town. The squire said we would stay overnight at a hotel. James drove us to the courtyard, and then took us to the stables.

  19. 19第 15 页

    After grooming, we were fed and got some rest. The stable grew dark and quiet. Suddenly, I awoke and began coughing. The loft was on fire! Then I heard someone cry, "Fire!"

  20. 20第 15 页

    James arrived and guided Ginger and me out, covering my eyes to calm me. James went back to save more horses as the stable was burning. We were safe, but two horses were lost. The fire was caused by a pipe, which reminded me of John's rule against fire near the stable. I wished all stables had the same rule to prevent such tragedies.

  21. 21第 15 页

    After our trip, James prepared to leave for his new job. Little Joe, a young and eager boy, arrived at the stable to take over James' role.

  22. 22第 16 页

    One night soon after James left, I was awakened by John. He asked me to go as fast as I could. Almost before I got to know what had happened, he got me fully prepared and led me out to see the squire. John got a note from the squire and set out for a doctor, for the mistress was badly ill.

  23. 23第 16 页

    After a long ride with the doctor, I was very tired. Little Joe wanted to take good care of me. However, due to lack of experience, he didn't give me anything to keep me warm but lots of cold water and a bucket of corn.

  24. 24第 16 页

    The squire learned I had a fever and he came to see me several times to thank me for saving his wife. John said that he had never seen a horse run so fast; it was like I knew what was wrong.

  25. 25第 17 页

    The mistress got sicker and the doctor came every day. The squire looked worried all the time. One day, John told Little Joe that the mistress needed to go to a warmer place for her health and the family was leaving soon. They did not know what would happen to them.

  26. 26第 17 页

    John and Little Joe looked even sadder. John said that this was just the beginning and soon everyone would be gone. He explained that the minister would take care of Merrylegs and promised to keep him forever. He added that Ginger and I would be sold to a friend of the squire's.

  27. 27第 18 页

    Ginger and I were led to our stalls, where we were groomed and given some hay. Later, John and Mr. York came back. Mr. York looked at us closely. He could tell that we were nice horses. John told him that I, the black one, had a perfect nature and had never been treated badly. He added that Ginger had a rough past, but she had been good for the past three years. Mr. York understood and mentioned that we would have to wear a checkrein, a strap to hold our heads up. It was a fashion that the lady demanded.

  28. 28第 19 页

    One day, the lady demanded that York raise our heads immediately. He tightened the checkrein on me so tightly that I could barely move. Ginger knew what was coming and started resisting. She reared up, accidentally hitting York in the nose. It was chaos as Ginger kept bucking and kicking. Eventually, she fell and kicked my hind leg in the process. York quickly intervened, sitting on Ginger's head to subdue her. People rushed to free her from the harness, and a groom released me.

  29. 29第 19 页

    Back in my stall, I felt upset and angry, with my leg throbbing from the kick. Ginger, tired and bruised, was led back to her stall.

  30. 30第 20 页

    When the weather turned warmer, the earl and his wife went to London. Mr. York went with the earl, and a senior groom named Reuben Smith took care of the stable. The earl's two oldest daughters, Lady Harriet and Lady Anne, stayed home with their brother and some visiting cousins during the summer.

  31. 31第 20 页

    When we reached the doctor's gate, Lady Anne asked Blantyre to go ahead while she and Lizzie would be waiting. Blantyre hesitated but eventually agreed. He hung my reins on the fence and disappeared into the trees.

  32. 32第 20 页

    On the other side of the road, Lady Anne and Lizzie stood calmly near an open gate leading to a field. Suddenly, a group of horses came trotting across the field, chased by a boy with a whip. Without warning, one of the horses rushed through the open gate and collided with Lizzie's hind end. Startled, Lizzie kicked strongly and dashed down the road.

  33. 33第 21 页

    I saw Lady Anne struggling to regain control as Lizzie raced down the road. I neighed loudly, showing my concern. Blantyre quickly returned and mounted me, letting me gallop freely as we chased after them.

  34. 34第 21 页

    Suddenly, Lizzie attempted to jump a wide ditch but stumbled on the other side, throwing Lady Anne off. Blantyre urged me to do my best, and I cleared the ditch with a powerful leap. He rushed to Lady Anne's side while I caught my breath.
