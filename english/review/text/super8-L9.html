  1. 1第 65 页

    One day, Mr. Fogg was waiting to meet his new servant. He told Mr. Fogg that his name was actually Jean Passepartout and that he was from France. He explained that he had done many different jobs in the past, like singing, teaching gymnastics, and being a fireman. But now, he wanted a quiet and peaceful life working for Mr. Fogg.

  2. 2第 66 页

    A card was left for him by Mr. Fogg; it listed the daily routine of the house. Passepartout surprisingly found that all of Mr. Fogg's clothes and shoes were marked with different numbers for proper seasons and occasions to be worn. He was happy with this quiet job.

  3. 3第 66 页

    Meanwhile, at the club where Mr. Fogg spent his evenings, he and his friends talked about a recent bank robbery while playing cards. One friend said the newspaper described the thief as a gentleman. Another friend disagreed, saying a person who steals a lot of money is no gentleman.

  4. 4第 66–67 页

    They also wondered where the thief could hide with all that money. One man thought the world was big enough for the thief to hide anywhere. But Mr. Fogg disagreed, saying that with modern transportation, it was now possible to travel around the world in just eighty days.

  5. 5第 67 页

    Suddenly one of the card partners declared that he would wager four-thousand pounds. All other players also agreed to participate. Mr. Fogg said that he had a deposit of twenty-thousand pounds at the bank and he would gladly risk on it.

  6. 6第 67 页

    The card partners felt excited about the bet, for they were quite sure they would win. However, they were also concerned about Mr. Fogg, as he was most likely to lose the money. But Mr. Fogg didn't regret making such a wager and as a decent gentleman, he kept his promises.

  7. 7第 68 页

    Without wasting a moment, Passepartout got the carpetbag packed. Mr. Fogg handed it to Passepartout and asked him to take good care of it because there was twenty-thousand pounds in it. With the railway and steamer timetable under his arm, Mr. Fogg left the house with Passepartout.

  8. 8第 68 页

    Mr. Fogg thought his departure from London would be big news. It was true. The extraordinary bet was known by more people and the news became the hottest topic in the newspapers around London.

  9. 9第 69 页

    Some people supported Mr. Fogg and believed his journey would be a success. But many thought that it was impossible for him to complete the journey in 80 days and he would end up in failure. Among the highly respected newspapers, only The Daily Telegraph supported his adventure.

  10. 10第 69 页

    The bank robbery still caught the attention of both the public and the police. A detailed description of the "gentleman" robber was reported and the police were looking for suspects all over Europe. Detective Fix was one of them and had been sent to Suez to look for the suspect. He watched with sharp eyes for any suspicious man who fit the descriptions of the thief.

  11. 11第 70 页

    Fix took the passport and read the description of its owner. To his great surprise, the description perfectly fit the bank robber. The man with the passport was Passepartout, and the passport in his hand belonged to Phileas Fogg. Fix suggested that his master should go to the consul's office in person. Passepartout mentioned that they were taking a long trip for a wager. He also disclosed his doubt that it was more than a wager. He told Fix that his master was rich and carrying brand-new banknotes with him. Fix was watchful and getting more interested in his boss.

  12. 12第 70–71 页

    The officer told Mr. Fogg that he didn't need a passport and that a visa would be useless. Mr. Fogg said he just wanted to show with a visa that he passed through Suez. The officer signed the passport with the date and the official stamp. Mr. Fogg paid the money and left politely with Passepartout behind him.

  13. 13第 71 页

    The first meeting with Mr. Fogg made Fix more suspicious. He was sure that Mr. Fogg was the man the police were looking for. It was his job to follow Mr. Fogg. Also, catching such a smart criminal would earn a big reward.

  14. 14第 71 页

    The telegram to the police in London brought great response from the public. Phileas Fogg was no longer a mysterious rich gentleman who had made a bet to travel around the world. Now his name filled the newspapers as a suspect of the bank robbery, wanted by the police, which was completely unknown to Mr. Fogg.

  15. 15第 72 页

    As soon as the ship stopped, Fix quickly went to the local police station to check whether the permission for Fogg's arrest had arrived. To his disappointment, the permission had not arrived yet. He asked the local police to issue an order to arrest Fogg, but the director refused and insisted that only London police could do that. Fix was determined to follow Mr. Fogg and always keep him in sight.

  16. 16第 72 页

    Passepartout had to finish his tasks quickly. Then he took a walk through the streets of Bombay, watching different people. Attracted by the beautifully dressed dancers, he followed them far from the center of the city. On the way to the station, he noticed a magnificent Indian temple on Malabar Hill. He did not know that Christians were forbidden to enter the temples and that even the Hindus must remove their shoes at the door. Those who broke these rules would be severely punished by the British government.

  17. 17第 72–73 页

    Passepartout was amazed by the decorations in the temple. Suddenly, three angry priests knocked him down and took his shoes. He didn't understand why they took his shoes and was very confused. In the end, he managed to get away and ran out of the temple.
